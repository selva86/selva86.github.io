Question 1 of 8

Mean versus median

A column of household incomes is right-skewed (a few very high earners). How do the mean and median compare?

Correct: extreme highs drag the mean above the more robust median.
The median is always defined.
Question 2 of 8

Reading a correlation

Two variables have a correlation of 0.9. The safest reading is:

Correct: r measures linear association, nothing more.
High r is consistent with, but does not prove, linearity.
Question 3 of 8

The 1.5 x IQR rule

The common boxplot rule flags a value as a potential outlier when it falls:

Correct: the fences sit 1.5 IQRs beyond the quartiles.
Half the data is above the mean; that is not an outlier rule.
Question 4 of 8

A spurious link

Ice-cream sales and drowning rates rise together across the year. The best explanation is:

Correct: a confounder produces the correlation without a direct link.
Reversing it is no more sensible.
Question 5 of 8

Taming skew

A strongly right-skewed variable is hard to model. A common first remedy is to:

Correct: a log pulls in a long right tail toward symmetry.
Rounding does not address skew.

What PCA finds

The first principal component of a dataset is:

Correct: PC1 captures the largest share of variance.
PC1 is the most informative direction.

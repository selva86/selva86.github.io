Question 1 of 8

Spotting untidy data

A sales table has one column per month, Jan, Feb, and Mar, each holding that month's revenue. By the rules of tidy data, what is the issue?

Right. You would reshape those three columns into a month column and a revenue column.
That would make the table less tidy, not more.
Question 2 of 8

A number that imports as text

You read a CSV and a price column arrives as text instead of a number. What is the most likely cause?

Yes. A single stray symbol in the column is enough for the reader to treat the whole column as text.
A clean numeric column imports as a number.
Question 3 of 8

The right verb for the job

You want to keep only the rows for the North region, with every column intact. Which verb does that?

Correct. filter keeps the rows that meet a condition and leaves the columns untouched.
summarise collapses rows into a summary, it does not subset them.
Question 4 of 8

mutate or transmute

What is the difference between mutate() and transmute()?

Exactly. Reach for transmute when you want just the derived columns and nothing else.
It works on columns of any type.
Question 5 of 8

What a grouped summary returns

You run group_by(region, quarter) and then summarise(total = sum(sales)). What does each row of the result represent?

Right. Grouping by two variables summarises every observed combination of them.
summarise collapses each group down to one row.
Question 6 of 8

How case_when resolves ties

Inside case_when(), a row satisfies two of your conditions at once. Which result does that row get?

Correct. Order matters, so put your most specific conditions first.
Overlap is allowed; the first match simply wins.
Run it: filter rows

Filtering rows in live R

Keep only the cars in mtcars with mpg of at least 25. Run the code below, then change 25 to 30 and run it again to watch the result shrink.

RInteractive R
library(dplyr) mtcars %>% filter(mpg >= 25)

  

  





That is filter at work: it keeps the rows meeting your condition and leaves every column in place.

Grouped summaries in live R



Find the average mpg for each cylinder count. Run it, then swap mean for max to get the best mileage in each group instead.




  

    
RInteractive R

    
library(dplyr)

mtcars %>%
  group_by(cyl) %>%
  summarise(avg_mpg = mean(mpg))

    

      
      
    

    

  

  





group_by then summarise is the workhorse pattern behind almost every report: one row out per group.

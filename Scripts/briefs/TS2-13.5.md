This chapter is a CAPSTONE PROJECT, not a tutorial. The reader must finish it
having watched a complete, industry-grade short-term energy load forecasting
engagement run end to end, at the standard a utility's analytics team would
present internally. Both a technical reviewer and a business stakeholder must
find it publish-worthy.

Mandatory arc (each phase is a top-level section with real code and real outputs):

1. BUSINESS BRIEF. A concrete engagement: a (realistic, fictional) regional
   utility or grid operator, the decision the forecast feeds (day-ahead
   scheduling / procurement), and the asymmetric cost of errors in currency
   terms (over-procurement vs peak shortfall). One short paragraph an
   operations planner could read alone.
2. DATA AUDIT. Use real half-hourly/hourly load data (vic_elec from tsibbledata
   or similar). Coverage, gaps, DST artifacts, outliers, and the sanity checks
   a practitioner runs before modeling sub-daily data.
3. RICH EDA. Minimum four publication-grade plots, each followed by an
   interpretation paragraph that changes a modeling decision: the multiple
   seasonalities (daily and weekly shape, gg_season at both scales), the
   load-temperature curve (its U shape / nonlinearity), calendar effects
   (workday vs weekend vs holiday profiles), and an STL decomposition with
   multiple seasonal windows. Figures carry titles, labeled axes, annotations.
4. FEATURE ENGINEERING. Temperature nonlinearity handled explicitly (degree
   days or a piecewise/quadratic term), lagged temperature, calendar features
   (weekday, holiday), and Fourier/harmonic terms for the seasonal cycles,
   each justified in one sentence.
5. STRATEGY PORTFOLIO. Fit at least FIVE genuinely different strategies, each
   introduced with WHY it suits sub-daily load: a seasonal naive benchmark
   (same time last week), harmonic regression (TSLM with Fourier terms +
   temperature), dynamic harmonic regression (ARIMA errors + Fourier +
   temperature covariates), an STL-decomposition hybrid (STL + ETS/ARIMA on
   the seasonally adjusted series), and a combination of the best performers.
   At least two strategies must handle multiple seasonality explicitly.
   Deterministic fits preferred; set.seed() before anything random.
6. HONEST TOURNAMENT. Time-series cross-validation with an expanding or
   sliding origin across several forecast days, compared at operational
   horizons (next day, next week). Rich results table (MASE, RMSE, MAPE by
   model and horizon) plus one comparison figure, read aloud in prose: who
   wins the peaks, who wins the shoulders, where the covariate models earn
   their keep. Include interval quality (empirical 80/95% coverage or Winkler),
   and show at least one day where the winner fails and diagnose why.
7. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY FOR STAKEHOLDERS. Readable in isolation: the
   recommendation in one sentence, quantified impact (MW error reduction
   translated into procurement cost), a results table in business language,
   the operating rule handed to the scheduling desk, and the top three caveats
   (temperature forecast dependence chief among them: the model consumes a
   WEATHER FORECAST in production, not observed temperature - quantify the
   sensitivity).
8. PRODUCTION NOTES. Monitoring (link to the Forecast Monitoring chapter),
   refit cadence, and what breaks first (new appliances, solar adoption,
   structural change - link to the structural breaks chapter).

Presentation rules: no output dump without an interpretation sentence; every
table is introduced and then summarized in prose; figures are styled for
publication, not defaults-only. The Complete Example section at the end
compresses the whole engagement into one continuous runnable script.

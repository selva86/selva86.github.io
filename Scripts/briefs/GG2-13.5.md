This chapter is a CAPSTONE PROJECT, not a tutorial: recreate THREE
professional-grade charts in pure ggplot2, at the standard of top newsroom
and business graphics, and teach the decisions while building.

Mandatory arc:
1. Pick three genuinely different published-chart STYLES (describe the style
   honestly rather than naming a masthead): (a) a dense small-multiples
   panel, (b) an annotated line chart that tells one story with direct
   labels and highlight color, (c) a diverging/comparison chart with a
   careful categorical layout. Use real public data for each.
2. For each: show the plan (what makes the original work: hierarchy, color
   restraint, annotation), then build it in layered steps with runnable
   code, then a side-by-side of default vs finished.
3. A "craft notes" section per chart: the three decisions that carried it,
   and the traps (dual axes, truncated axes, rainbow palettes) avoided.
4. Everything reproducible: fonts handled with fallbacks so the code runs
   in the browser; the polished versions exported at print quality.
5. Close with the reusable checklist: 12 questions to ask before any chart
   ships.
Presentation: this is the book's showpiece; every figure must look
genuinely publication-grade in the rendered page.

What this credential attests

Every r-statistics.co credential is earned, not awarded. To hold one you work a track's exercise hubs to mastery, then pass a timed, code-based assessment: you write real R that an automated grader runs against a battery of correctness checks. It either works, or it does not.

That is what gives the credential its weight. It attests to demonstrated skill, performed under conditions that cannot be faked or guessed, and every credential carries a public verification page, so a recruiter or hiring manager can confirm it in a single click. It is the receipt for work already done.

06

Straight answers

Does it cost anything?

It depends on the track. The two foundation credentials, R Fundamentals and Tidyverse Practitioner, are free to earn, with the same public verification page and Open Badge as any other. The three career-track credentials, Data Visualization, Statistics and Machine Learning, are part of Pro. There is never a separate proctoring fee or exam voucher.

Why are some credentials free and others Pro?

The foundations are free so anyone can see the credential is real and earn one at no cost. The career-track credentials, the ones hiring managers care most about, sit inside Pro. That keeps the highest-value signal behind real commitment, which is part of what makes it worth holding.

So what is Pro for, then?

Pro is where the three career-track credentials live: Data Visualization, Statistics, and Machine Learning. It also carries the things around the work, progress that follows you across devices, your full attempt history, and a dashboard across every track. The tutorials and the two foundation credentials stay free.

How is the assessment graded?

It is code, not multiple choice. You write real R that an automated grader runs against a battery of correctness checks, then scores on what the code actually does. There is no option to pick from a list, so the result reflects skill rather than recognition.

Do I have to solve every exercise in a track?

You need at least 80% of a track's exercises solved across its hubs before the assessment unlocks. Anything you cracked earlier, even before you decided to chase the track, counts toward that 80%.

What does "verifiable" actually mean?

Each credential gets a unique ID like RST-2026-A7K3F9 and a public page anyone can load to confirm the holder, track, score and issue date. It is also emitted as Open Badges 3.0 JSON signed by the r-statistics.co issuer, so it imports into badge wallets and LinkedIn.

Can a credential be revoked?

Yes. In rare cases of an integrity violation, a credential can be revoked, and its public page then shows that status. That possibility is part of why the credential is worth holding in the first place.

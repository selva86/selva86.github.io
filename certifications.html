What this credential attests

Every r-statistics.co credential is earned, not awarded. To hold one you complete a roadmap track: at least 80% of the graded checks across its lessons, each one passed by writing real R that the grader runs and checks. It either works, or it does not.

That is what gives the credential its weight. It attests to skill shown across a whole track of graded exercises, and every credential carries a public verification page, so a recruiter or hiring manager can confirm it in a single click. It is the receipt for work already done.

06

Straight answers

Does it cost anything?

It depends on the track. Certified R Fundamentals is free to earn, with the same public verification page and Open Badge as any other. The Data Analyst and specialization certificates come with Pro: Single Track covers Data Analyst plus your chosen specialization, All-Access covers every track. There is never a separate proctoring fee or exam voucher.

Why are some credentials free and others Pro?

New to R is free so anyone can see the credential is real and earn one at no cost. The career-track credentials, the ones hiring managers care most about, sit inside Pro. That keeps the highest-value signal behind real commitment, which is part of what makes it worth holding.

So what is Pro for, then?

Pro opens every section of the Data Analyst track and the four specializations, and with them their certificates. The tutorials, the exercise hubs, the New to R track and its certificate stay free.

How are the exercises graded?

It is code, not multiple choice. You write real R, and the grader runs it and checks the output against the expected result. There is no option to pick from a list, so the result reflects skill rather than recognition.

Do I have to pass every check in a track?

You need at least 80% of the graded checks across a track's published lessons before you can claim the certificate. Checks you passed earlier count toward that 80%. Researcher and R Developer certificates open once those tracks are fully published.

What does "verifiable" actually mean?

Each credential gets a unique ID like RST-2026-A7K3F9 and a public page anyone can load to confirm the holder, track, score and issue date. It is also issued as Open Badges 3.0 JSON naming r-statistics.co as the issuer, and adds to LinkedIn in one click.

Can a credential be revoked?

Yes. In rare cases of an integrity violation, a credential can be revoked, and its public page then shows that status. That possibility is part of why the credential is worth holding in the first place.

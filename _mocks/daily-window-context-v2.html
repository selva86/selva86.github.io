What the null really says

The null is a claim about the world, not about your data

The null hypothesis is the boring explanation: the coin is fair, the drug does nothing, the two groups came from the same population. It is a statement about the process that generated your sample, not about the sample itself.

That distinction is what makes the whole machinery work. If the null were a claim about your 40 observations you could simply look at them and settle it. Because it is a claim about the process, you have to ask a different question: if the boring explanation were true, how often would a sample like mine turn up?

Worth pausing on
Every test you will meet answers that one question. What changes from test to test is only how the word "like" is measured.

A note from the desk

I sat on certificates for years because most of them are theatre. You pay, you click through a multiple-choice quiz, you get a PDF that proves you can recognise the right answer when it is one of four. That tells a hiring manager almost nothing, and they know it.

So this one works the other way around. There is no question you can guess. You write actual R, it runs against tests you cannot see, and it either passes or it does not. The certificate is just the receipt for work you already did. If you can hold it up, it means you can do the thing.

05

Straight answers

Does it cost anything?

No. Earning a certificate is free. You solve the exercises, pass the assessment, and it mints automatically. No proctoring fee, no exam voucher, no upsell at the finish line.

So what is Pro for, then?

The tutorials and the certificate are free, and they always will be. Pro is for the things around the work: progress that follows you across devices, your full attempt history, and the dashboard that tracks every track at once. You never have to pay to be certified. You pay, if you want, to make the journey more comfortable.

How is the assessment graded?

It is code, not multiple choice. You write real R that runs against hidden test cases and is auto-graded. There is no answer key to recognise, which is exactly the point.

Do I have to solve every exercise in a track?

You need at least 80% of a track's exercises solved across its hubs before the assessment unlocks. Anything you cracked earlier, even before you decided to chase the track, counts toward that 80%.

What does "verifiable" actually mean?

Each certificate gets a unique ID like RST-2026-A7K3F9 and a public page anyone can load to confirm the holder, track, score and issue date. It is also emitted as Open Badges 3.0 JSON signed by the r-statistics.co issuer, so it imports into badge wallets and LinkedIn.

Can a certificate be revoked?

Yes. In rare cases of an integrity violation, a credential can be revoked, and its public page then shows that status. That possibility is part of why the credential is worth holding in the first place.

D2 accents: six professional marks

Box colors are locked to D2 (7% field, 11% hover, full-hue accents, monogram, certificate hairline). Each panel below changes only the graphic mark that replaces the dot, so you are choosing an identity accent, nothing else. Certificate rows now reserve two lines on every card, so box heights stay uniform even where a long certificate name wraps.

E0 · Bare

No object at all. The D2 field, monogram, certificate line and nothing else — the baseline every accent below must beat. The color story is carried entirely by the tinted field and the full-hue monogram, name hierarchy does the rest.

Trade-off: nothing distinctive to remember it by; this is the panel that felt "lacking" in earlier rounds, now with the locked D2 tuning.

E1 · Chamfer corner

A solid fold of full color clipped into the top-right corner. The most architectural of the six: reads as a filing tab or flagged document, gives every card a decisive block of saturation with zero ornament, and grows slightly on hover. Common in enterprise design systems precisely because it never looks decorative.

Trade-off: the most austere; there is no motif to "get", just geometry.

E2 · Spine rail

A 4px full-hue rail along the left edge. The classic editorial accent — reports, ledgers, callouts — and the calmest way to put full saturation on every card. The rail turns each box into a labeled entry in a system, which quietly reinforces "structured path".

Trade-off: the most conventional of the six; dozens of well-designed products use exactly this.

E3 · Rising bars

A small ascending bar chart in the track hue. Four bars stepping upward, fading in as they rise; they stretch slightly on hover. This is the accent that belongs to a statistics site — the mark IS the subject matter, it implies growth, and no generic design system produces it. My pick of the six.

Trade-off: it is an illustration, however minimal; if you want pure geometry, E1 is stricter.

E4 · Halftone fade

A dot matrix dissolving out of the corner. The dot idea professionalized: instead of one balloon, a fine grid of full-hue points fades diagonally out of the top-right, like a halftone or a scatterplot dissolving into the page. IBM built an entire design language on this device. It still whispers "R." without shouting it.

Trade-off: the subtlest color mass of the six; on small screens the fine dots blur toward texture.

E5 · Arc

An open ring in the track hue, top-right. A circular arc drawn to ~80% with a light remainder — the visual language of progress and completion, which is exactly what a roadmap promises. Rotates a quarter-turn on hover, which makes the panel feel quietly kinetic.

Trade-off: an arc at a fixed angle can be misread as a real progress meter showing data it doesn't have; if that bothers you, the full closed ring is the honest variant.

E6 · Ghost watermark

The track initials set huge as a translucent watermark. Oversized 900-weight initials in the full hue at 13% opacity, bleeding off the top-right like a magazine folio mark; they deepen slightly on hover. The most editorial of the six — annual-report elegance, and the color arrives as a large soft field rather than a small solid object.

Trade-off: the initials duplicate the monogram square (a deliberate motif repeat, but a repeat); the low opacity means less punch than E1 or E3.

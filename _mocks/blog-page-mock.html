Linear Regression in R: The Complete Guide

Linear regression models the relationship between a numeric outcome and one or more predictors by fitting a straight line that minimizes squared error — the foundation of statistical modelling in R.

SP Selva Prabhakaran • Updated 19 May 2026 • ✓ Reviewed for accuracy • 🕑 14 min read Intermediate
Synced to your account — resume on any device

Linear regression is where statistical modelling begins. Before random forests and neural networks, there is the humble straight line — and understanding it well makes every model after it easier. In this guide you'll fit, read, and check a linear model in R, running every snippet right here as you go.

Fitting your first model

R's lm() function fits a linear model. The formula syntax y ~ x reads as “y explained by x.” We'll use the built-in mtcars dataset to predict fuel economy (mpg) from weight (wt).

Rlinear-regression.R
# Fit a simple linear model
model <- lm(mpg ~ wt, data = mtcars)
summary(model)
#> Coefficients:
#> Estimate Std. Error t value Pr(>|t|)
#> (Intercept) 37.2851 1.8776 19.86 < 2e-16 ***
#> wt -5.3445 0.5591 -9.56 1.3e-10 ***
#> Multiple R-squared: 0.7528

The wt coefficient of −5.34 means each additional 1,000 lbs of weight is associated with about 5.3 fewer miles per gallon. The R-squared of 0.75 says weight alone explains 75% of the variation in fuel economy — a strong single predictor.

💡
Reading the stars. The *** next to a coefficient marks a p-value below 0.001 — strong evidence the predictor matters. Always pair significance with effect size, not just the asterisks.

Interpreting the coefficients

Every linear model answers two questions for each predictor: how big is the effect, and how certain are we of it. The table below maps each part of the output to its meaning.

OutputMeaning
EstimateThe slope — change in y per unit of x.
Std. ErrorUncertainty around the estimate.
t valueEstimate divided by its standard error.
Pr(>|t|)Probability of seeing this effect if the true slope were zero.

Checking the assumptions

A fitted model is only trustworthy if its assumptions hold: linearity, constant variance, and roughly normal residuals. R makes this a one-liner — plot(model) draws four diagnostic charts. Practice reading them in the exercises linked below.

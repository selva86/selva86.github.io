Confidence Interval Calculator

i New to confidence intervals? Read the 4-min primer ▾

What it is. You measure something on a small sample (50 customers, 30 patients, a handful of widgets) and you want to know what the truth is for the whole population. A confidence interval gives you a range of plausible values for that truth, not a single guess.

How to read it. A 95% CI of (67.9, 76.1) means: if we collected fresh samples like this 100 times and computed a CI each time, about 95 of those intervals would contain the truth. It does not mean "the truth is in this interval with 95% probability", that's a Bayesian credible interval, a different beast.

Picking the flavor. The right CI depends on what you're measuring. Numeric values from a sample → use a CI for the mean. Yes/no rates → CI for a proportion. Two groups compared → CI for a difference. Pick the matching flavor in the sentence below.

        Anatomy of a confidence interval
      
      
    
        
    
          
    CI = estimate ± critical · SE
    
          
    The general form. Most CIs decompose into a point estimate, a critical value from a sampling distribution (z, t, χ²), and a standard error. The critical depends on the confidence level and (for some flavors) df; the SE comes from the data.
    
        
    
        
    
          
    SE_mean = s / √n
df = n − 1
critical = t1−α/2, df
    
          
    Mean (one sample). If you don't know σ, and you almost never do, use the t-distribution. For n > 30 the t and z critical values are essentially identical.
    
        
    
        
    
          
    Wilson score CI for p:
center = p̂ + z²/(2n)
half = z·√(p̂(1−p̂)/n + z²/(4n²))
CI = (center ± half) / (1 + z²/n)
    
          
    Proportion. Wilson is the right default. It stays inside [0, 1], performs well near the boundaries, and matches prop.test(..., correct = FALSE).

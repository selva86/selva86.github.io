Roadmap progression · 3 directions

Climb, climb-then-branch, or equal tracks

Same syllabus in all three. The only thing that changes is the dominant axis: whether the picture is mainly about how far you have climbed (level), about a shared climb that then opens into specializations, or about four equal destinations with no top. Flip between them and notice which question each one answers first.

1. A real climb

levels are the whole story

Beginner at the bottom, advanced at the top. The eye reads one thing instantly: how far you have come and how much is left. Specializations exist, but they live at a level rather than off to the side. Certificates are the gates between tiers.

Beginner tiers (shared) Advanced tiers Certificate gate

Why it works

It is the only one of the three that actually answers "how far do I have to go." The staircase makes effort and reward legible, the "you are here" marker gives a logged-in user a real position, and certificate gates feel earned because they sit on the risers between tiers.

Watch out

It re-introduces the hierarchy you were nervous about: ML / Time Series / Stats get compressed into one "Level 3" band, which can read as "specializations are a detail" rather than four real destinations. Best when level genuinely matters more than choice of track.

2. Climb, then branch

progression first, fork second

A genuine leveled climb through the shared foundation, gated by your first certificate, that then opens into the four tracks. It reads as a progression first and a choice second, which is the honest order: nobody picks Time Series before they can write a function.

Why it works

Best of both: the left column is an unambiguous beginner-to-confident climb with a real gate, and the right column keeps the four tracks visibly parallel and equal. It scales to a clickable nav (each track expands to its stages) and the stage pips already hint at depth and progress.

Watch out

Two reading directions in one figure (down the climb, then right into the fork) needs the dashed seam to stay obvious, or it looks like two unrelated widgets. The single most important detail to get right in build.

3. Equal tracks

shared base, no top

The original instinct, polished: a foundation band everyone shares, then four side-by-side lanes with no level axis at all. It answers "what can I become" rather than "how far have I climbed." Strong if the priority is making every specialization feel equally valid and avoiding any "beginner" stigma.

Why it works

Cleanest and most scannable, scales to real lesson detail, and the four roles sit on parallel cards rather than a ladder. No beginner ever feels "below" anyone.

Watch out

It is the one that is not really a progression. There is no sense of climb, distance, or "you are here," which is exactly what felt missing before. Reads as a menu, not a journey.

Random Forests in R

A random forest averages hundreds of decorrelated decision trees. The art is in the tuning, and you'll do it on a real model below, not just read about it.

Intermediate45 min14 exercisescontains mathUpdated Jun 2026

How a forest makes a prediction

Each tree Tb is grown on a bootstrap sample, splitting on a random subset of mtry features at each node. The forest's prediction is the average across all B trees, which cuts variance without raising bias:

$$\hat{f}_{\text{rf}}(x)=\frac{1}{B}\sum_{b=1}^{B} T_b(x), \qquad \widehat{\text{err}}_{\text{OOB}}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n} L\!\left(y_i,\ \hat{f}^{(-i)}(x_i)\right)$$
Out-of-bag error averages each point's prediction over only the trees that did not see it, a free validation set.

Key insight

The OOB curve flattens, so more trees never hurt accuracy, only runtime. But mtry is a real trade-off: too small and trees underfit (high error), too large and they become correlated (variance creeps back). The sweet spot is near √p.

Behind the scenes

A live model needs care: the R session is pre-warmed before you arrive, the demo runs on a 2,000-row sample so a re-fit returns in under a second, and a skeleton shows while a heavier "Run on the full data" job streams back. That is how interactivity survives a real ML workload.

Linear Regression in R: The Complete Guide

Linear regression models the relationship between a numeric outcome and one or more predictors by fitting a straight line that minimizes squared error.

SPSelva Prabhakaran •Updated 19 May 2026 • Reviewed • 14 minIntermediate
Synced to your account

Linear regression is where statistical modelling begins. In this guide you'll fit, read, and check a linear model in R, running every snippet right here as you go. (Tip: select any sentence to highlight it.)

Fitting your first model

R's lm() function fits a linear model. The formula y ~ x reads as “y explained by x.” We'll predict fuel economy (mpg) from weight (wt) using the built-in mtcars data.

Rlinear-regression.R
# Fit a simple linear model
model <- lm(mpg ~ wt, data = mtcars)
summary(model)
#> Coefficients:
#> Estimate Std.Error t value Pr(>|t|)
#> (Intercept) 37.2851 1.8776 19.86 <2e-16 ***
#> wt -5.3445 0.5591 -9.56 1.3e-10 ***
#> Multiple R-squared: 0.7528

The wt coefficient of −5.34 means each extra 1,000 lbs is associated with ~5.3 fewer mpg. An R-squared of 0.75 says weight alone explains 75% of the variation in fuel economy.

Reading the stars. The *** marks a p-value below 0.001, strong evidence the predictor matters.

Interpreting the coefficients

Every model answers two questions per predictor: how big the effect is (the Estimate), and how certain we are of it (the Std. Error and p-value).

Checking the assumptions

A fitted model is only trustworthy if linearity, constant variance, and roughly normal residuals hold. plot(model) draws four diagnostic charts, practise reading them in the linked exercises.

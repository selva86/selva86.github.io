"Pick a learning track": three designs for the six boxes

Same six tracks, same real numbers (sections, lessons, free sections, certificate). The wordy paragraphs are gone from all three; the difference is what carries the value: a promise line, a showcase, or the roadmap itself as a chart.

Option A: Three-line cards

Closest to boot.dev's own path cards. Each box is a name, one promise line, and one line of numbers. Scannable in five seconds; the detail lives on the roadmap page. Safest choice; least distinctive.

Pick a learning track

Six roadmaps, 674 lessons, seven certificates. Each one takes you from the first line of code to the person who understands the model

Option B: One track at a time

A rail of six tracks on the left, one showcase panel on the right. Only the selected track's detail is visible, so the section stays short while every track gets its full pitch: promise, a bar of its sections with the free ones in green, four hidden-gem chips, the numbers, two buttons. Interactive, and it makes the free sections visible at a glance. Most persuasive; needs a click to see the others.

Pick a learning track

Six roadmaps, 674 lessons, seven certificates. Green sections are free.

Option C: The roadmap as a chart

Six rows, and the visual is the data: every section of every track drawn as a segment, free ones in green, lessons and certificate at the right. Compact, honest, and it looks like something a statistics site would make. Distinctive; the least "card-like", so it reads as a table on small screens.

Option C, five variants

Four distinct evolutions of the certificate-forward card panel, plus your pale-button specification as C5. All seven real tracks, per-track native colors, Inter Tight throughout. Hover everything; C5 responds to press.

C1 · Sealed

The card IS the certificate. Each card carries an embossed wax-seal check in the track color and closes with a signature line: the certificate's name in bold italic, "verifiable" in the corner. On hover the card acquires the track-color frame. Quietly premium; the credential is the design language itself.

Trade-off: the seal motif must stay subtle or it tips into skeuomorphism; least informative about lesson volume.

C2 · Progress cards

A product, not a brochure. Monogram + level tag up top, real stats (sections · lessons), and a segmented meter in the track color — showing the signed-in user's actual progress, or the track's scale for visitors. Hovering slides up a full-width "Start free" action bar. The menu itself feels like the platform.

Trade-off: needs the progress wiring to fulfil its promise; densest information load of the five.

C3 · Split-tone

Color does the sorting. A pale track-color band crowns each card with the monogram and level; the white body stays calm with name, line, and a certificate footer whose name takes the track color. The eye groups by hue before reading a word — the fastest scan of the card family, and the band structure fixes raggedness by design.

Trade-off: seven hues in one panel demands the pale tints stay pale, or it turns carnival.

C6 · Full-tone (C3, your revisions)

Split-tone without the split. Whole-card track tints with NO border line at all: the tint alone defines each box, corners sharp, and the boxes keep their breathing room. Hover lifts the card with a shadow cast in its own hue. The certificate footer keeps its same-hue hairline.

Trade-off: with full tints, the seven hues sit shoulder to shoulder; the tint level (11%) is tuned so adjacency stays pleasant, but it deserves one squint test on a real monitor.

C7 · The existing layout, C6's coloring

Nothing moves, everything gains color. The current dropdown's exact structure: header, the three stage columns (Start, Work with data, Go deep), per-card lesson meta and Start-free links, footer. Each card takes its track's 11% tint with sharp corners and no border line, keeping the original spacing between boxes, and the Start-free link picks up the track's deep tone. The cheapest to ship of every variant: pure CSS on the existing markup.

Trade-off: inherits the existing layout's raggedness (the Start column holds one card, so white space sits below it, now more visible against the tinted stacks).

C4 · Featured + rail-cards

Hierarchy over democracy. New to R gets a full-width featured card (tinted, with its own CTA) because that IS the right answer for most visitors; the other six become compact horizontal rows, two per line, each with monogram, one-liner, and a pale cert chip. The panel gets ~30% shorter while gaining a clear recommendation.

Trade-off: deliberately un-equal — the six compact tracks get less selling space; wrong choice if all tracks matter equally to strategy.

C5 · Pale buttons (your specification)

Track boxes as honest buttons. Each box wears a pale, pleasant tint of its track's native color (9% fill, deeper edge), sharp 2px corners, and a true button anatomy: a thicker bottom edge that compresses on press — click it and it physically depresses before navigating. Name in the track's deep tone, lessons count and certificate inside. Unmistakably clickable, pleasantly tactile.

Trade-off: the button metaphor trades away hover-reveal subtlety; the seven tints must be tuned once against each other so none screams (the violet and pink run hotter than the greens).

Recommendation

C3 for shipping now; C2 as the destination once progress wiring exists. Split-tone gives the strongest instant-scan and professional polish with pure CSS. C2 is the deepest product experience but only earns its meter once it shows real progress. C5 delivers the most unmistakable affordance of the five and pairs surprisingly well with C4's featured-row idea if you ever want a hybrid. C1 is the mood piece: pick it if the certificate story becomes the site's headline.

The R Roadmap · art direction

Four ways to draw the same path

All horizontal, all on one baseline, nothing raised above anything. Same content, same type and palette as the approved design. Only the diagram changes. Track cards and the capstone band below it stay exactly as you saw them.

A. The line

timeline + rails

A single horizontal timeline carries the shared foundation to your first credential, then runs on as four parallel rails. The progression reads strictly left to right; the four rails sit at the same scale and weight, just different colors.

left to right · foundation, credential, four parallel rails

Why it works

The cleanest, lightest read. One continuous line establishes the progression, the rails make "four equal directions" literal, and the stage dots and end seals hint at depth and reward without clutter. Ages well as a hero.

Watch out

Thin lines carry little detail. Great as the top-of-page overview; the track cards underneath do the heavy lifting.

B. The stepper

chevron chain

A horizontal process chain: the shared foundation advances block by block to the credential, then a flat bracket opens onto four equal track tiles in a single row. Solid, product-like, and unmistakably a sequence.

a sequence of blocks, then four equal tiles

Why it works

Reads instantly as a process: do this, then this, then pick. The four tiles are equal rectangles in one row, so no track outranks another. Carries more detail per element than the line.

Watch out

Boxier and more "UI" than editorial. The bracket connecting credential to tiles must stay crisp or it looks like two stacked widgets.

C. The lanes

horizontal gantt

One shared foundation column on the left; four equal lanes run rightward from it, each a sequence of stages ending in a certificate. Every lane starts at the same point and uses the same bar, so depth is shown horizontally and no lane sits higher.

shared start, four equal lanes running right

Why it works

The most informative: it shows the actual stages of every track at a glance, all starting from the same foundation. This is the one that doubles as real, clickable navigation and a logged-in progress view.

Watch out

Densest of the four, so the typography and spacing have to stay disciplined. Less of a single "wow" image, more of a working map.

D. The ribbons

flowing bands

The foundation enters as one solid band, passes through the credential, and flows out as four equal-weight ribbons that level off and run straight to the right. The most expressive, motion-suggesting option while staying flat.

one band in, four equal bands out

Why it works

The most distinctive and brand-able. Solid bands feel like momentum and flow, the four are visibly equal in width, and the white notches read as stages. Strong hero with personality.

Watch out

The most decorative, so it carries the least text. Best paired tightly with the detailed track cards directly beneath it.

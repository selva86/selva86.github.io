# The Ideal R Roadmap, v2: from curriculum to product

> Companion to `ideal-r-roadmap-2026.md` (v1). v1 is the curriculum. v2 is the
> experience, monetization, and positioning layer on top of it. v1 answers "what
> should they learn." v2 answers "why will they feel wow, and why will they pay."
> v1 is not replaced; this builds on it.

---

## 1. Honest scorecard of v1

| Dimension | Score | Why |
|---|---|---|
| As a **curriculum** (rigour, coverage, sequencing) | **8 / 10** | Thorough, modern, well sequenced. A genuine professional path. |
| **Delight / wow factor** | **5 / 10** | Reads like a syllabus. Complete, but a list of topics does not create emotion. No first-impression hook, no momentum, no shareable wins, no aspiration made tangible. |
| **Compulsion to subscribe** | **3 / 10** | v1 has almost no monetization design. It never says what is free, what is paid, why anyone pays, or at what moment. The certificate is named but not made desirable. |
| **As a product** (the thing that earns money) | **~4 / 10** | A strong skeleton with no nervous system. |

**The core diagnosis:** v1 designed the *content* and assumed the *product* would
follow. It will not. Delight and conversion are deliberately engineered, not a
byproduct of good content. A free, comprehensive curriculum with no engineered
value ladder is a beautiful library that no one pays to enter, because the door is
already open.

---

## 2. The gaps, named

**Delight / wow gaps**
1. **No first-10-minutes hook.** Nothing that makes a stranger feel "I can do this"
   in the first session. The climb map (now live) is the start; it needs a
   "run your first real R in 60 seconds" moment beside it.
2. **No visible momentum.** Progress, streaks, and XP exist in the app but the plan
   never uses them as the emotional engine ("you are 70% to your first certificate").
3. **No shareable wins.** Nothing the learner is proud to post. No "I built this,"
   no badge to share at each milestone, no portfolio artifact.
4. **The certificate is named, not desired.** It is listed as an endpoint, not built
   up as an aspiration the learner wants from minute one.
5. **No celebration.** No milestone moments, no "you just cleared Level 2."

**Conversion gaps**
6. **No free-vs-paid model at all.** The single most important missing piece.
7. **No conversion moments.** Nowhere does the design say "this is where, and why,
   someone decides to pay."
8. **The paid layer is thin and undesigned.** Certificates and "project briefs" are
   mentioned, but not as a product worth a subscription.
9. **No urgency or scarcity** mechanics tied to the founding-cohort pricing.
10. **No social proof or credibility** woven into the path.

---

## 3. The answer to "enough free, enough gated?"

**Yes on free. No, not yet, on gated.** v1 has abundant free content (about 90
lessons, every exercise hub, every tool). That is correct: free content is the SEO
moat, the top of funnel, and the brand promise ("every tutorial, tool and exercise
is free, and stays free"). Do not gate it.

What v1 is missing is a **designed paid layer** strong enough to convert. The split:

| Layer | What it is | Free | Pro / Certificate |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Learn** | All lesson pages, runnable code, explanations | Free, unlimited | - |
| **Practice** | All exercise hubs, auto-grading, XP, streaks | Free, unlimited | - |
| **Tools** | Every calculator, picker, interpreter | Free, unlimited | - |
| **See the path** | The roadmap, the climb map | Free | - |
| **Track (light)** | In-browser + free-account progress, save, reading position | Free account | - |
| **Prove** | The level **mastery assessment** (code + concept, pass-once) | Try, ungraded preview | **Graded, gated** |
| **Credential** | The **verifiable certificate** per level (public URL, LinkedIn, Open Badges) | - | **Pro** |
| **Guided mode** | Structured checkpoints, "X% to your next certificate," personalised next step | - | **Pro** |
| **Projects + review** | The per-level project brief, an auto/guided review, a public **portfolio page** | Brief is free | **Review + portfolio = Pro** |
| **Worked solutions** | Full walkthroughs for the **advanced** exercise tiers | Beginner solutions free | **Advanced solutions = Pro** |
| **Downloads** | Cheat sheets, certificate PDFs, project templates, datasets | Sampler | **Full set = Pro** |
| **Capstone** | Certified R Data Scientist, awarded for all six | - | **Pro** |

**Principle:** free is everything you need to *learn*. Pro is everything that
*proves it, keeps you moving, and turns the work into something you can show*. The
free tier never feels crippled; the paid tier sells the three things free cannot
give: **proof, momentum, and an outcome you can hold up.**

This split is defensible because none of the paid items cannibalise SEO or the brand
promise, and each maps to a real willingness-to-pay (employers value credentials;
quitters pay for accountability; everyone wants a portfolio).

---

## 4. The conversion engine: how free effort becomes a paid credential

The model is not "hit a paywall." It is "**invest free effort, watch it compound
into visible progress toward a credential you now want, then pay to claim the proof
and keep the momentum.**" The funnel:

1. **Land -> wow.** Climb map + "run your first R now." Feel: *this site has got it,
   and I can start immediately.*
2. **Learn + practice free.** Lessons and exercises accrue XP and progress. The
   roadmap visibly fills in. Feel: *I am moving.*
3. **First milestone (free).** Clear a stage, get a small shareable badge and a
   "you are 35% to the R Fundamentals certificate" nudge. Feel: *the certificate is
   close and real.*
4. **The assessment gate (conversion moment 1).** At the end of a level the mastery
   assessment is the natural next step. Preview is free; claiming the graded result
   and the certificate is Pro. The learner has already done the work; paying is the
   completion, not the cost.
5. **The portfolio pull (conversion moment 2).** The project brief is free; the
   review and the public portfolio page (the thing you put on a CV) is Pro.
6. **The streak/accountability pull (conversion moment 3).** For people who fear
   quitting, Pro is the structure and accountability that gets them to the summit.
7. **The capstone aspiration (conversion moment 4).** Six certificates building to
   "Certified R Data Scientist" is a goal worth subscribing a year for.
8. **Urgency (conversion moment 5).** Founding-cohort price locks at sign-up;
   September launch gives a real date.

The key insight: **the credential-led model converts on sunk effort, not on
withheld content.** Every free lesson the learner finishes increases the pull of the
paid proof. So generous free content is not a leak; it is the conversion fuel.

---

## 5. Delight and wow mechanics to add

1. **The 60-second win.** Next to the climb map: a single runnable block that
   produces a real chart or model, with "you just did this in R." Instant capability.
2. **Momentum made visible.** The roadmap shows the learner's real progress filling
   the climb, per level, with a personal "you are X% to <certificate>."
3. **Milestone celebrations.** A small, tasteful moment when a stage or level is
   cleared, plus a shareable badge image.
4. **Shareable artifacts.** Each level ends in something postable: a badge, a
   certificate, a portfolio entry, the project output itself.
5. **The certificate as aspiration from minute one.** Show the (sample) certificate
   early and often; let people see exactly what they are climbing toward.
6. **A portfolio page per learner.** Public, clean, "here is what I have built and
   proven in R." This is the single strongest wow-plus-conversion artifact.
7. **Social proof in context.** Learners-certified counts, alumni, real
   testimonials, placed at the certificate and assessment moments, not just the
   pricing page.
8. **Tasteful scarcity.** Founding-member framing and the launch date, shown where
   the decision is made.

## 6. Top 4 positioning strategies

Ranked, with the recommended primary first. They are complementary; lead with one,
reinforce with the rest.

**1. Credential-led: "Free to learn. Pay to prove." (PRIMARY)**
The content is free and stays free; the product is a verifiable, code-based
certificate you earn by solving real problems. Positions sharply against two enemies
at once: watch-and-forget video courses, and pay-for-a-badge certificate mills. This
is already the brand; v2 sharpens it into a funnel. Headline energy: *"Don't just
learn R. Prove you can do it."*

**2. Outcome / portfolio-led: "Finish with proof of work, not just a certificate."**
Every level produces a shareable artifact and feeds a public portfolio page (a
forecast, a model notebook, a published package). Sells the tangible outcome and the
career signal, and gives the wow ("I built this"). Strong with career-changers.

**3. Structure-and-momentum-led: "The library is free. The climb is Pro."**
Reframes the free tutorials as the (overwhelming) library and Pro as the guided
climb: sequenced checkpoints, visible progress, accountability, and the thing that
actually gets people to finish. Sells *finishing*, which is what most self-learners
secretly fail at. This is the day-to-day retention hook.

**4. Employer-trusted rigour: "A certificate a hiring manager can verify."**
Lead on the credibility of the assessment (code plus concept, pass-once, publicly
verifiable URL, Open Badges, LinkedIn). Positions the credential as trustworthy
signal, not participation. Strong for B2B/team sales later.

**Recommended mix:** lead the page and the brand with **#1**; use **#3** as the
in-product hook that drives daily return and conversion on sunk effort; use **#2**
and **#4** as proof points and CV-moment closers. Avoid a cohort/community position
for now (the site is solo-authored; community is a future track, not a launch claim).

---

## 7. What changes on the roadmap page and site

- **Above the climb:** a one-line value prop in the #1 voice + a 60-second runnable
  win + a "what is free vs Pro" two-line clarity strip (honest, not a paywall tease).
- **On each level chapter:** when signed in, the real progress bar and "X% to
  <certificate>"; a small sample-certificate thumbnail; the project artifact named.
- **At each checkpoint:** the certificate made concrete (preview, verify-URL idea,
  LinkedIn), with the assessment as the clear next action.
- **At the summit:** the capstone as the year-long goal, with founding-cohort framing.
- **Everywhere:** progress, XP and streak surfaced (they already exist in the app),
  plus milestone badges and a link to the learner's portfolio page.
- **Free/Pro legibility:** a tiny, consistent marker on gated items (assessment,
  certificate, advanced solutions, project review) so the line is always honest and
  never a surprise.

## 8. Target scores after the revamp, and what to measure

| Dimension | v1 | v2 target |
|---|---|---|
| Delight / wow | 5 | 8 to 9 |
| Compulsion to subscribe | 3 | 7 to 8 |
| Product overall | 4 | 8 |

**Leading metrics:** roadmap -> first-exercise rate; exercises solved per new user
in week 1; stage-completion rate; assessment-preview starts; preview -> paid
conversion; portfolio pages created; certificate share rate. **Lagging:** founding
subscriptions, renewal, capstone completions.

---

## 9. The one-paragraph summary

v1 built a world-class *curriculum*. It is not yet a *product* because it never
designed the free-to-paid value ladder, the conversion moments, or the delight
mechanics. The fix is not to gate the content; it is to make free learning visibly
compound into a credential people want, and to build the paid layer (assessment,
certificate, guided momentum, project review, portfolio, capstone) into something
worth a subscription. Free gives you everything to *learn*; Pro gives you *proof,
momentum, and an outcome you can show*. Lead with "free to learn, pay to prove,"
reinforce with momentum and portfolio, and the same generous free content that powers
SEO becomes the fuel that converts on sunk effort.

## C. Full Section Outline

### Lead Sentence
Pre-registration is a time-stamped, public commitment to your hypotheses, methods, and analysis plan — filed before you see the data, so your results can't influence the analysis you report.

### Introduction (2-3 paragraphs)
- Hook: Imagine finishing an analysis and realizing you could get a significant result by tweaking one filter. Pre-registration prevents that temptation.
- What it is: a written specification of hypotheses, methods, and analysis plan, registered publicly before data collection
- Why it matters: prevents p-hacking, HARKing, and selective reporting; increasingly required by journals and funders
- What you'll learn: how to write a pre-registration, use R tools to create one, compare OSF vs AsPredicted, handle deviations transparently

### Common Mistakes (3-5)
1. Writing vague hypotheses that can fit any result
2. Forgetting to specify exclusion criteria
3. Not pre-registering the exact statistical test (just "we will use regression")
4. Treating pre-registration as all-or-nothing (failing to disclose deviations)
5. Pre-registering after data collection (defeats the purpose)

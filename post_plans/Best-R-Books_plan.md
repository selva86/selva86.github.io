## C. Section outline

**Lead sentence:** Most R book lists are undifferentiated 20-book dumps. This one is ranked, opinionated, and tells you exactly when to skip a book — because no one reads 20 books.

**First H2 opening plan** (≤80 words motivating prose for the first question):
> R has a book problem that most languages envy: there are too many *good* books, most are free online, and several are written by the people who built the tools. The risk isn't finding a bad book — it's reading five overlapping ones. The fix is to pick one entry point, finish it, then specialise. This list walks that path.

### Core sections (question-form H2s)

1. **## Why does R have so many good free books?**
   - R's academic origin and the R Core / Posit publishing culture
   - Why so many authoritative books are free online (bookdown, CRC Press arrangements)
   - How to tell "still-maintained" from "abandoned" (check GitHub edition date, package version notes)
   - Callout: [KEY INSIGHT] — choose one beginner book, finish it, then branch by goal

2. **## Where should a complete beginner start?**
   - *R for Data Science (2e)* — the canonical starting point; what it covers; who should skip it
   - *Hands-On Programming with R* — for readers who want R as a programming language, not just a data tool
   - *Learning Statistics with R* — for readers who also need the stats
   - Short comparison table: book → best for → skip if
   - Callout: [TIP] — Read R4DS 2nd edition, not 1st — native pipe and modern tidyverse

3. **## Which book teaches modern data wrangling best?**
   - *R for Data Science (2e)* — chapters 4-6 are the dplyr / tidyr core
   - *Practical Data Science with R* — business-oriented, real messy data
   - *R Cookbook (2e)* — problem-indexed reference, not a teaching book
   - When to use each
   - Callout: [NOTE] — dplyr 1.1+ superseded `_at`/`_if`/`_all` with `across()`; prefer books published 2022+

4. **## What's the best book for R visualisation?**
   - *ggplot2: Elegant Graphics for Data Analysis (3e)* — the grammar, by Wickham
   - *R Graphics Cookbook (2e)* — recipe-indexed, fastest path to "how do I make this chart?"
   - *Fundamentals of Data Visualization* (Wilke) — not R-specific but uses R; design principles
   - When to read the grammar vs. grab a recipe
   - Callout: [TIP] — the Graphics Cookbook is the reference you'll keep open; the grammar book is the one you read once

5. **## Which books will make you an expert R programmer?**
   - *Advanced R (2e)* — metaprogramming, environments, OO systems; the book that makes you *understand* R
   - *R Packages (2e)* — building and shipping reusable code; paired with Advanced R
   - *The Art of R Programming* (Matloff) — software-design angle, lower-level than Wickham
   - Callout: [WARNING] — Advanced R is not an intro; attempting it before 6 months of R will frustrate you

6. **## Which books cover statistics and machine learning in R?**
   - *An Introduction to Statistical Learning* (ISLR) — the free textbook the field standardised on; now has an R lab edition
   - *Hands-On Machine Learning with R* (Boehmke & Greenwell) — applied, tidymodels-adjacent
   - *Tidy Modeling with R* (Kuhn & Silge) — the tidymodels canon
   - *Statistical Rethinking* (McElreath) — Bayesian inference; the best-written stats book of the last decade
   - Callout: [KEY INSIGHT] — ISLR teaches you *what* to do; Statistical Rethinking teaches you *why*

7. **## What about specialised domains — time series, text, Bayesian?**
   - *Forecasting: Principles and Practice (3e)* — Hyndman; free; the time-series standard
   - *Text Mining with R* — Silge & Robinson; tidytext workflow
   - *Bayesian Data Analysis* (Gelman et al.) — the heavy reference
   - *Geocomputation with R* — Lovelace et al.; geospatial
   - When to reach for each

8. **## How do I pick the right book for *my* goal?**
   - Reading-path diagram (Figure 1) referenced here
   - Decision framework in prose: data-analyst vs. statistician vs. R developer vs. ML engineer
   - Rule of thumb: one book per stage, finish it, then move on
   - Callout: [TIP] — buying versus reading free online — when to pay

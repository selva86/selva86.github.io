## Lead sentence

R is best when statistics or research is your core work; Python is best when deep learning or production software is your core work — and the public survey, job-market, and ranking data from 2024-2026 agree on exactly that split, despite a decade of heated debate.

## First H2 opening plan (≤80 words)

For years, "R vs Python" threads have run on vibes. You can actually settle most of the debate with four public datasets: the Kaggle ML & DS Survey, the TIOBE Index, the PYPL Index, and Glassdoor's 2025/2026 salary data. Let's pull the numbers into a small tibble and plot them, so you can see the gap with your own eyes instead of taking anyone's word for it.

### Summary
Bullet list of 5-6 takeaways:
- Python wins on sheer adoption + deep learning + production
- R wins on statistics + visualization + pharma/academic research
- Both wins are real; neither is "dying"
- Benchmarks are a library story, not a language story
- The right pick depends on your goal, not someone else's tribe
